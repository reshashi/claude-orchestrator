## Top 5 Savings Opportunities

### 1. Breast Cancer Treatment Audit — $22,400 to $44,800/year

A female employee in her 50s is undergoing active breast cancer treatment. Total cost over 12 months: **$223,998**. This includes $86,024 in specialty infusions (oncology medications administered in a clinical setting).

**What a line-by-line audit would look at**:
- Are infusions being administered in the most cost-effective setting? Hospital outpatient infusion centers charge 2-4x more than physician office or home infusion.
- Are there duplicate charges, unbundled procedures, or billing errors in $191K of medical claims?
- Could reference-based pricing apply to facility charges?

**Why this matters**: Cancer treatment billing is the #1 area where we find recoverable savings. The complexity of oncology billing (drug administration codes, facility fees, physician fees, lab work) creates more opportunities for billing errors than any other condition.

Cigna's care management flagged this member for Complex Case Management. That's about managing her care path. **No one is auditing her bills.**

### 2. GLP-1 Drug Trajectory — $10,150 to $20,310/year

Eight months ago, no one at Noveon was taking Mounjaro. Today, 4 employees are on it at a cost of **$1,085 per prescription**. Combined with Ozempic, Trulicity, and Rybelsus, your GLP-1 spend has reached **$67,700 annualized** across 7 members.

**The trajectory**:

```
Base Period:    Mounjaro $0          Total GLP-1: ~$25K annualized
8 months later: Mounjaro $24K/8mo    Total GLP-1: ~$68K annualized
Projected 2027: Mounjaro $50K+       Total GLP-1: $100K+ (if unchecked)
```

**What can be done**:

- Step therapy: Are members trying first-line treatments before jumping to $1,000/month drugs?

**Why this matters**: GLP-1 drugs are the fastest-growing cost category in employer health plans nationwide. Your hypoglycemics class grew **106% in 8 months**. Without intervention, this trajectory doesn't flatten.

## Something Your Broker Should Have Told You

### You're Self-Insured — And That Changes Everything

Your health plan is structured as a **level-funded arrangement with Cigna** at a 50% funding level with pooled stop-loss coverage. In plain English: **you are self-insured**.

This means:
- **You own the claims data.** Every claim paid on behalf of your employees flows through your account. Cigna is the Third-Party Administrator (TPA), not the insurer.
- **You have the legal right to access your raw claims data.** Under ERISA (the Employee Retirement Income Security Act), plan sponsors of self-insured plans have the right to their own claims data.
- **Cigna must provide it upon request.** A signed authorization from you as plan fiduciary is all that's required.

### Why This Matters

The utilization report you receive from your broker is a **summary**. It tells you where to look. Raw claims data tells you **what's actually wrong** — individual billing errors, overcharges, duplicate claims, and pricing anomalies that are invisible at the summary level.

With your raw claims data, we can run a full 7-layer audit that examines:

| Layer | What It Checks |
|-------|---------------|
| Billing Integrity | Duplicate charges, unbundled procedures, upcoding |
| Price Variance | Charges vs. Medicare rates, geographic variance |
| Network Integrity | Surprise billing, balance billing |
| Medical Necessity | Appropriate care setting, frequency |
| Plan Compliance | Cost sharing accuracy, prior authorization |
| Episode Analysis | Cost outliers, fragmented care |
| Pattern Detection | Provider anomalies, rising trends |

### What We Need From You

A signed authorization (included with this report) directing Cigna to provide your claims data to MedicalBills.com, along with a standard Business Associate Agreement (BAA) for HIPAA compliance. The authorization takes 5 minutes to sign and typically 2-4 weeks for Cigna to fulfill.
